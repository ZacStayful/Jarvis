## Identifying the mandatory objections for this business

Pull the objections list from:

1. **Sales call transcripts** — the questions asked in the first 10 minutes
2. **Support tickets from the first 30 days** post-purchase
3. **Competitor FAQ sections** — objections their readers raise
4. **PAA boxes** for the primary keyword cluster
5. **Live chat / contact form** patterns

A typical business has 4–7 mandatory objections. Fewer than 4 means you've missed some. More than 7 means you've split similar objections that should be combined.

## Classification guidance

### Critical class — body copy required

Objections that:
- Decide the click vs. bounce moment
- Get raised in the first 30 seconds of a sales call
- Are the dominant reason someone hesitates to commit

These MUST appear in body copy (not buried in FAQ). Usually 2–3 of the total set.

### Standard class — FAQ acceptable

Objections that:
- Get raised after the initial interest is established
- Are common but not deal-breakers
- The reader can find by scrolling to FAQ

Usually 3–4 of the total set.

### Edge case — brief note acceptable

Objections that:
- Apply to a small subset of readers
- Don't appear in 80%+ of sales calls
- A 1–2 sentence acknowledgement is sufficient

Usually 0–2 of the total set.

## Objection cascade — order matters

Objections come up in a sequence in the reader's mind. The page addresses them in approximately this order:

1. [First objection — usually the most fundamental "does this work?" question]
2. [Second — the "how do I know?" question]
3. [Third — the "what's the catch?" question]
4. [Fourth — once the reader is considering acting]
5. [Fifth — the final risk question]
6. [Sixth — the final friction question]

## How to verify the list is complete

After drafting the list, run these checks:

1. **Sales call check:** pull 5 recent sales call recordings. In the first 10 minutes, every question asked should map to one of the listed objections. If a question doesn't map, either the question is rare (skip) or the list is incomplete (add).

2. **PAA check:** search the primary keyword. Every PAA question that gets raised should map to one of the listed objections, or the FAQ section should answer it as a Standard-class entry.

3. **Competitor check:** scan 3 competitor service pages. Note their FAQ sections. Every objection their FAQs address should either be on your list or deliberately omitted.

4. **First-pass conversion check:** if you have access to user testing or session recordings, watch 3 sessions. Note every point the user paused, re-read, or bounced. Those moments correspond to unaddressed objections.

## Refresh cadence

Quarterly:
- Pull recent sales call samples
- Pull recent support tickets
- Check whether PAA has shifted
- Update the objections list

Annually:
- Full re-derivation from scratch
- Compare to current list
- Add new objections, retire any that have become non-issues
