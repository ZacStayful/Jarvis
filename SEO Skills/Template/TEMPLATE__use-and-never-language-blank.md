## FAQ question format rules

These rules apply to every FAQ:

1. Always use the lead's phrasing verbatim where possible
2. Never use business jargon in the question trigger
3. PAA questions from Google must appear verbatim
4. One question per FAQ item (never combine)
5. Never start an FAQ answer with "Great question" or any affirmation
6. Start FAQ answers with the direct answer, then the explanation

---

## FAQ answer format — example

### Wrong

> Q: [Question]
>
> A: That's a great question! At [Business], we pride ourselves on [feature]. Our team works tirelessly to ensure [outcome]. Typically, [actual answer].

### Right

> Q: [Question]
>
> A: [Direct answer first]. [Then the mechanism or context]. [Then any necessary caveat.]

The right version: direct answer first, then the mechanism (what determines or qualifies the answer). No affirmation, no padding.

## How to source lead language

If you don't yet have a documented lead language list:

1. Pull live sales call transcripts — what words do leads use?
2. Pull live chat transcripts — even shorter, questions in raw form
3. Read support tickets from the first 30 days post-purchase
4. Read PAA boxes for primary keyword
5. Read forum threads (Reddit, industry forums) — closest to true lead language

Build the tables. Update quarterly.

---

## Why this matters

Every FAQ in lead language:
- Matches PAA boxes verbatim
- Extracts cleanly for AI Overviews
- Surfaces more often in voice search
- Reads as if you understand the reader

Every FAQ in business language:
- Misses PAA matching
- Reads as corporate marketing
- Feels distant to the cautious reader
- Reduces conversion silently

The cost of getting this wrong is invisible until you compare conversion rates between businesses that get it right and those that don't.
