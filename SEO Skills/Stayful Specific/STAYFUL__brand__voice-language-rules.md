# Stayful — Voice and Language Rules

**Scope: Stayful Airbnb-management overlay.** Copy style, the positioning statement, use-this-language list, and never-use-this-language list. Universal framework: `CORE__04_frameworks__objections-and-faq-language-framework.md`.

---

## The single goal of every page

**Get a cautious, practical UK property owner to type in their postcode and request an income estimate.**

Every word on every page either serves that goal or it doesn't. The test for any sentence: would removing it make the reader less likely to request the estimate? If no, the sentence is overhead.

---

## Who is reading

A cautious, practical UK property owner with a specific property in mind, quietly anxious about making the wrong call.

**The "not ready yet" visitor:** Roughly half of interested owners are 2–6 months from being ready. Frame the estimate as something you do *before* you're ready: "Even if your property isn't available yet, running the numbers now means you go into the decision with real figures."

---

## Emotional state — every page must do three things before selling

1. **Acknowledge the decision they're facing**
2. **Reduce uncertainty** — give specific, honest numbers including slower months
3. **Build calm confidence** — not hype, not urgency tactics

Tone: calm, commercially clear, and reassuringly specific.

---

## The five copy jobs every page must do — in order

1. **Acknowledge** — make the reader feel this page is written for their exact situation
2. **Compare** — position short letting clearly against the alternative
3. **Quantify honestly** — give specific net income figures including slow months
4. **Reassure on risk** — address what happens if occupancy is lower than expected
5. **Prove** — 4.8 star Google rating, anonymised owner examples, real occupancy data

---

## Copy style

Professional but approachable. Editorial prose, not bullet-point AI copy. Named local demand drivers in every city page. Specific named figures throughout. Never generic.

### Expert insight layer — mandatory on every page

At least three pieces of content per page that could only come from genuine local or operational knowledge:

- A named local demand driver specific enough to be verifiable
- A specific occupancy or rate figure referenced to a comparable property type in that postcode
- An honest acknowledgement of a genuine local risk or seasonality pattern
- A named event, development or employer with a direct line to short-let demand
- An observation about local guest profile specific to that market

Generic statements about "strong local demand" or "popular tourist destination" do not qualify.

---

## The positioning statement

> Stayful is not an Airbnb management company selling a service. For this audience, Stayful is: **a better property decision for owners who want more income and more flexibility than a standard tenancy, without taking on more work or uncertainty alone.**

Every page should support this positioning. The page is the better decision; the conversion is the next step in making it.

---

## Use this kind of language

| Phrase | Why |
|---|---|
| "What you'd actually keep each month after fees and costs" | Frames net, not gross |
| "Including the quieter months" | Honest framing |
| "A hands-off option for owners who don't want the day-to-day" | Names the value (time freedom) |
| "Based on live local comparables, not best-case projections" | Trust signal |
| "We handle everything — guests, cleaning, pricing, maintenance coordination" | Specific, not vague |
| "Monthly income paid directly to you between the 1st and 5th of each month" | Specific commitment |
| "Even in a slower year, this property would typically net £X" | Worst-case + reassurance |
| "No STL provider can honestly guarantee a fixed monthly income — including us" | Honest disqualifier |
| "You block dates in your owner calendar — no approval needed" | Control reassurance |
| "40% of our bookings come direct — not through Airbnb" | Asymmetric proof |

---

## Never use this kind of language

| Phrase | Why |
|---|---|
| "Maximise your RevPAR" | Industry jargon |
| "Supercharge your short-term rental income" | Hype |
| "Scale your Airbnb portfolio" | Wrong audience tone |
| "Unlock the full potential of your asset" | Empty marketing speak |
| Peak projections without a full-year or worst-case figure | Trust collapse later |
| Any phrasing that implies a guaranteed income floor | Fabrication, will fail |
| Any phrasing that implies the worst case cannot happen | Same |
| Sanitised business language in FAQ questions | Lead-language rule violation |

---

## Why this voice works

The target audience is property owners who have been over-marketed-to for years. They've heard "maximise your income" from every Airbnb management pitch deck since 2018. The voice that wins is the one that doesn't pitch — that gives them specific, honest, useful information and lets the data speak.

Calm, commercial, specific. Every sentence sounds like advice from someone who actually manages properties, not someone selling management.

---

## Common voice failures

### Failure 1 — Marketing-speak by default

The default writer instinct produces "premium service", "professional management", "expert team". All correct, all useless to a cautious reader. Replace with specific facts.

### Failure 2 — Hype words

"Skyrocket", "supercharge", "maximise", "unlock". Every appearance is a trust signal failure.

### Failure 3 — Generic local copy

"Sheffield is a vibrant city with strong tourism demand." Could be any city. Replace with specifics: named demand drivers, specific occupancy figures, named seasonality patterns.

### Failure 4 — Pitch-deck phrasing in body copy

"Our innovative platform connects you with..." Body copy should read as analysis, not as a pitch deck slide.

### Failure 5 — Empathy without specificity

"We understand how stressful being a landlord can be" — empathy with no information. Replace with empathy + specifics: "If your last tenant left a £4k repair bill, you already know why landlords are leaving long-term tenancies. Here's what short-letting looks like instead."
