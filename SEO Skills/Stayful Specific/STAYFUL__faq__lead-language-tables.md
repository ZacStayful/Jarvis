# Stayful — FAQ Lead Language Tables

**Scope: Stayful Airbnb-management overlay.** The full "use this / not this" tables for every category of FAQ question.

Universal principle: `CORE__04_frameworks__objections-and-faq-language-framework.md`.

---

## Why these tables matter

Every FAQ question must be written in the language of the lead, not the language of the business.

The right phrasing matches PAA boxes, extracts cleanly for AI Overviews, and reads like genuine conversation. The wrong phrasing — business jargon — misses all three.

## Income and revenue questions

| Use this (lead language) | Not this (business language) |
|---|---|
| "What if I have a really bad month?" | "What happens during periods of lower occupancy?" |
| "Can you guarantee how much I'll earn?" | "What income assurance does Stayful provide?" |
| "How do I know these figures are accurate?" | "How are income projections calculated?" |
| "What's the worst case?" | "What is the minimum expected annual return?" |
| "Am I going to earn more than with a long-term tenant?" | "How does STL compare to AST income?" |

## FAQ question format rules

1. **Always use the lead's phrasing verbatim** where possible
2. **Never use business jargon** in the question trigger
3. **PAA questions harvested from Google must appear verbatim**
4. **One question per FAQ item** — never combine two questions into one trigger
5. **Never start an FAQ answer with "Great question"** or any affirmation
6. **Start FAQ answers with the direct answer**, then the explanation

---

## FAQ answer format — example

### Wrong

> Q: How quickly can you get it listed?
>
> A: That's a great question! At Stayful, we pride ourselves on our fast and efficient onboarding process. Our team works tirelessly to ensure your property is listed on all major platforms in record time. Typically, our onboarding process takes 7–14 days.

### Right

> Q: How quickly can you get it listed?
>
> A: 7–14 days from your onboarding call to live listings on Airbnb, Booking.com, VRBO and Google. The variance comes down to photography scheduling and how much existing setup is already in place.

The right version: direct answer first (7–14 days), then the mechanism (what determines speed). No affirmation, no padding.

## How to extend these tables

For a new question category not in the tables above:

1. Pull 5–10 real lead phrasings of the question (sales calls, support tickets, chat logs)
2. Identify the common phrasing pattern
3. Compare to the business-language phrasing that would be the default writer instinct
4. Add to the table

The tables are a living document. Update quarterly as language shifts.

## Common failures

### Failure 1 — Business-language fallback under pressure

When writing fast, the default phrasing creeps in. "What is the onboarding process?" instead of "How quickly can you get it listed?" Run every FAQ trigger through the lead-language test before publish.

### Failure 2 — Combined questions

"What if a guest damages something and how do you handle bad reviews?" Two questions. Split.

### Failure 3 — Generic FAQ openings

Every FAQ should be a different angle. Avoid the temptation to have multiple "what does it cost" framings.

### Failure 4 — Missing PAA questions

If Google's PAA box for the primary keyword shows a question, that question must appear in the FAQ verbatim. The Phase 1 audit's PAA harvest step ensures this.

### Failure 5 — Padding in answers

"Great question" / "Thanks for asking" / "We're glad you asked" — all violate the answer-first rule. Delete every affirmation.
