# Stayful — Proof Levers and Disqualifier

**Scope: Stayful Airbnb-management overlay.** The six proof points Stayful uses across every page, and the disqualifier that functions as both honesty signal and CTR tool.

Universal framework: `CORE__04_frameworks__worst-case-framing-and-proof-points.md`.

---

## The six proof levers

### 1. The income estimate itself

The page's primary conversion. The estimate produces a specific, postcode-tailored figure that the reader could not get elsewhere. The conversion IS the proof — running the form delivers value before any commitment.

### 2. Anonymised case studies — always with a slower month figure

One per page maximum. Format:

> "Owner, [property type], [area] — previously £[X] long-let, now nets £[Y] average / £[Z] best month / £[W] slowest month."

Always include the slowest month. A case study without a slow-month figure reads as marketing.

### 3. The direct booking strategy — as a frame, not a stat

Frame as:

> "The estimate shows you year-one net income. The direct booking strategy is how that figure improves over time."

This is more compelling than the bare statistic ("40% of bookings come direct"). It points the reader to what the figure means for them specifically.

### 4. Specific guest demand types

Hospital staff, contractors, university visitors, event attendees — named specifically per city.

Generic "tourists" doesn't qualify. Specific demand types named with their typical booking pattern do qualify.

### 5. The 4.8 star Google rating

Reference naturally in body copy, not just as a badge. Example:

> "Our 4.8 star rating on Google — most reviews from owners after their first full quarter, when the income figures are real."

The mechanism (when owners review) gives the rating more weight than the number alone.

### 6. The 40% direct booking figure

**Always explain the mechanism, not just the statistic.**

Mechanism: "We list on five platforms — Airbnb, Booking.com, VRBO, Google and Stayful direct. Over time, direct bookings replace platform-routed bookings, reducing platform dependency and improving net income."

The figure without the mechanism is a marketing claim. The figure with the mechanism is structural proof.

---

## How to choose proof points

For any Stayful page, pull 2–4 proof points from the six. Not all six need to appear on every page.

| Page type | Recommended proof points |
|---|---|
| City management page | 2, 4, 5, 6 |
| County hub page | 2, 4, 5, 6 |
| Income calculator page | 1, 2, 5 |
| Guide page | 5, 6 + topic-specific proof |
| Switching / comparison page | 2, 6 + structural comparison |

---

## The mechanism rule — applied

Every statistic must be accompanied by the mechanism that produces it.

| Statistic | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| "65–70% average occupancy" | "Above the 55% AirDNA market average for managed UK STR properties" |
| "40% direct bookings" | "Listing on 5 platforms; direct bookings grow over time, reducing platform dependency" |
| "4.8 star rating" | "Most reviews from owners after their first full quarter, when the income figures are real" |
| "70+ properties managed" | Less mechanism needed — scale is self-explanatory |
| "£3M+ delivered to hosts" | "Across managed portfolio since [year], net of fees and costs" |
| "7–14 day onboarding" | "Variance from photography scheduling and existing setup state" |

Mechanism turns marketing claims into structural facts. The reader who understands WHY a figure exists trusts the figure more.

## The disqualifier — exact wording

> "If you need a guaranteed fixed amount each month regardless of bookings, short letting may not be the right fit."

Use exactly as worded.

Place once on the page, near the primary CTA.

### What the disqualifier does

1. **Honestly disqualifies** a reader for whom short-let is wrong
2. **Implicitly qualifies** the readers for whom short-let is right
3. **Reduces bounce** from poorly-fit visitors
4. **Increases conversion** from well-fit visitors (they feel directly addressed)
5. **Builds trust** by demonstrating the business won't take just anyone

The disqualifier is a costly signal — Stayful would gain (short-term) from taking every visitor. Volunteering "we're not right for you if [X]" is what builds long-term trust.

---

## When to add more disqualifiers

For pages targeting specific profiles where additional honest disqualification helps:

| Profile | Additional disqualifier |
|---|---|
| Profile D (selling) | "If your property is on a residential mortgage and listed for sale, short-letting typically won't be profitable enough to justify the setup." |
| Profile E (moving abroad) | "If your property has a residential mortgage rather than a holiday let / BTL mortgage, short-letting may not be permitted by your lender." |
| Profile F (rent-to-rent) | "If you're operating a rent-to-rent on a managed portfolio, the combined fee load typically exceeds the achievable margin." |

These additional disqualifiers go in FAQ, not body copy. They're not for the primary profile B reader.

## Common failures

### Failure 1 — Proof stacking without mechanism

"4.8 stars, 70+ properties, £3M+ revenue, 40% direct, 65–70% occupancy" — a wall of statistics. Each has more weight when explained individually with its mechanism.

### Failure 2 — Case study without a slow-month figure

The case study reads as marketing without the slow-month. Always include it.

### Failure 3 — Generic "as featured on" logo strip

The "trusted by" logo strip is the least valuable proof element. Replace with one specific case study and the Google rating with mechanism.

### Failure 4 — Disqualifier omitted

"We help every landlord!" approach. Increases bounce, reduces conversion from ICP, hurts long-term reputation.

### Failure 5 — Disqualifier as a footnote

Buried in FAQ or a closing paragraph. The disqualifier should be visible near the form, where it does the qualifying work.

---

## How proof levers compound

A page with 2 strong proof levers (with mechanism) outperforms a page with 6 proof levers (no mechanism).

Stacked statistics with no explanation are filler. Statistics with explained mechanisms are proof.

Density matters less than depth.
