# Stayful — Worst-Case Framing Language

**Scope: Stayful Airbnb-management overlay.** The five worst-case framing rules applied to Stayful income copy, with the exact phrasings.

Universal rules: `CORE__04_frameworks__worst-case-framing-and-proof-points.md`.

---

## Rule 1 — Always show worst case alongside typical, never in isolation

**Correct format:**

> "In a slower year, this type of property in [city] typically nets £X per month. A comparable long-term tenancy would pay £Y. Even in the quieter period, short-term letting typically remains the stronger financial outcome."

Four components:

1. The worst-case figure (£X)
2. The comparison alternative (£Y)
3. The honest framing ("even in the quieter period")
4. The structural conclusion ("typically remains the stronger financial outcome")

All four must appear together.

---

## Rule 2 — Never fabricate a floor or imply a guarantee

The worst-case figure must be a real worst-case observed in actual data, not a marketing-derived "we never see worse than" claim.

If Stayful's data shows occasional outcomes below the worst-case quoted, the worst-case figure must be lowered or qualified.

---

## Rule 3 — Follow worst-case with structural reassurance

After stating the worst case honestly, **immediately** explain why below-market performance is structurally unlikely.

Two parts:

1. **Stayful consistently beats market average occupancy** (65–70% vs. 55% AirDNA average)
2. **The 40% direct booking figure reduces platform dependency**

### Exact language to use

> "Below-market performance would require two things to fail at once: the pricing and occupancy expertise we apply to every property, and the direct booking channel that currently accounts for 40% of our bookings."

Use exactly as worded.

---

## Rule 4 — Never lead with worst case

The order is always:

1. Typical figure
2. Worst-case figure
3. Structural reassurance
4. Comparison to alternative

Leading with worst case creates a frame the reader can't escape. They never see the typical figure as the baseline; they see it as the optimistic claim.

---

## Rule 5 — The income estimate is the honest anchor

### Exact language to use

> "The estimate shows you what this property type typically earns in your postcode, including what quieter months look like — not just the peak figure."

This re-anchors the entire page's promise: honest data, not marketing projections.

---

## Where each phrasing goes

| Phrasing | Page position |
|---|---|
| Rule 1 four-part framing | First mention of income figures (Zone 2, income comparison panel) |
| Rule 3 structural reassurance | Immediately after every worst-case mention |
| Rule 5 honest-anchor framing | Near the income estimate form (top CTA and bottom CTA) |

---

## The full Objection 2 statement (income guarantee)

> "We don't guarantee a fixed income figure — and we'd be cautious of any company that does. What we show you is the realistic range, including quieter months, based on comparable properties in your postcode. Even in a slower year, the net figure typically exceeds what a long-term tenancy would pay."

Place near the income estimate form. This statement does the five things:

1. Denies the guarantee
2. Frames competitors who guarantee as untrustworthy (costly signal)
3. Refocuses on the realistic range
4. Acknowledges the worst case
5. Provides structural comfort

---

## Common failures

### Failure 1 — Worst case in isolation

"In a slower month, this property would net £X." Sounds like a confession; reader anchors on the bad number.

Fix: always include typical + comparison + structural reassurance.

### Failure 2 — Fabricated worst case

A worst-case figure better than actual observed data. Eventually fails when a real customer experiences worse. Trust collapses.

### Failure 3 — Worst case followed by sales push

"In a slower year, this property nets £X. Run your estimate now!" The transition feels like deflection. Insert structural reassurance BEFORE any CTA prompt.

### Failure 4 — Multiple worst-case figures stacked

"In a slow month £X, in a slow quarter £Y, in a slow year £Z." The reader anchors on the lowest number. Show ONE worst-case figure (typically the slow-year annual net), with appropriate context.

### Failure 5 — Paraphrasing Rule 3 structural reassurance

The "two things would need to fail at once" framing is specifically engineered. Paraphrases ("we use expert pricing and have direct bookings") lose the structural force. Use the exact wording.
