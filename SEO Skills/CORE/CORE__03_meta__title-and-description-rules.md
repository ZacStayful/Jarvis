# Meta Title and Description Rules

**Scope: Universal.** The meta title and description are the first thing a reader sees before clicking. They get more views than the rest of the page combined. Their rules are stricter than body copy.

---

## Meta Titles

### The primary rule: keyword first, hook second

**Structure:** `[Primary keyword] — [Specific promise or curiosity gap]`

- **Weak:** "Airbnb Management Leeds — Stayful" (keyword + brand name = label)
- **Strong:** "Airbnb Management Leeds — What Your Property Could Realistically Earn"

The brand name is the lowest-value element in a title. Replace it with a specific promise or a curiosity gap whenever possible.

### Character limit and truncation rule

Write all meta titles at **55 characters or under**.

The keyword and the most important element of the hook must both appear before character 55. Anything beyond 55 may be truncated by Google in some viewports.

### Profile-matched title construction

Different reader profiles respond to different title constructions. Match the title to the page's primary profile.

**Profile A (existing operator seeking management):**
- "[City] Short Let Management Company — [X]% Occupancy, Everything Handled"
- "Airbnb Management [City] — Switch Management Company Without Losing Momentum"

**Profile B (existing landlord considering switch from long-let):**
- "Short-Term Letting [City] — Is It Worth More Than Your Current Long-Let?"
- "Holiday Let Management [City] — Honest Income Figures Including Slower Months"

**Profile C (purchasing for STL):**
- "Short-Let Income [City] — What a Property Like Yours Realistically Earns"
- "Buy to Short Let [City] — Net Income Figures From 189 Verified Properties"

**National pages:**
- "Airbnb Management Company UK — 15% Fee, 40% Direct Bookings, No Setup Cost"

### The title writing test

Read the title as if you are a reader who typed the primary keyword and is now scanning ten results. Ask:

> Does this title imply something specific that the others do not?
> Does it create a gap between what I know now and what I would know if I clicked?

If the answer to either question is no, rewrite the title before moving to the description.

### Meta title reference table by page type

| Page type | Primary profile | Title structure | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| City management page | B | [Keyword] — [income comparison or decision framing] | "Airbnb Management Nottingham — What Your Property Earns vs a Long-Let" |
| City management page | A | [Keyword] — [performance signal or differentiator] | "Short Let Management Nottingham — 65–70% Occupancy, Everything Handled" |
| National service page | B/A | [Keyword] — [differentiator + specific figure] | "Holiday Let Management UK — 15% Fee, No Setup, 40% Direct Bookings" |

| County hub page | B | [Keyword] — [county-level income signal] | "Holiday Let Management Yorkshire — What Properties Earn Across the County" |

## Meta Descriptions

### The primary rule: moral contract first, detail second

The first sentence alone — read in isolation — must give a reader a reason to click.

- **Weak first sentence:** "Stayful is a UK-wide Airbnb management company covering [City]."
- **Strong first sentence:** "See what a [City] property like yours typically earns on short-term letting — including slower months."

The strong version is a **moral contract**: "If you click, you'll find this specific thing." The weak version is a brand introduction — useful information, but not a reason to click.

### Character limit rule

Write all meta descriptions at **150 characters or under**.

Google may extend descriptions to ~155 characters in some viewports but compresses below 130 in others. 150 is the safe target.

### The disqualifier as a CTR tool

A description that briefly acknowledges the page is not right for everyone increases CTR from the right people and reduces bounce from the wrong ones.

- **Without disqualifier:** "Find out how much your [City] property could earn on short-term letting."
- **With disqualifier:** "If you're weighing up whether short-term letting beats your current long-let in [City] — this page gives you the honest comparison."

The "if you're [specific situation]" opening qualifies the click. Readers in that situation feel directly addressed. Readers not in that situation self-select out — reducing bounce.

### Profile-matched description construction

**Profile A:** "Stayful manages short lets in [City] at 15% + VAT — no setup fee. 65–70% average occupancy. See what a switch looks like for your property."

**Profile B:** "Wondering if short-term letting beats your [City] long-let? This page shows the honest net income comparison — including what a slower month actually looks like."

**Profile C:** "Conservative short-let income estimates for [City] — net figures from 189 verified property enquiries."

**National:** "Full-service Airbnb management at 15% + VAT. 40% of bookings come direct — not through Airbnb. Managing 70+ properties across the UK."

### The description writing test

Read the **first sentence** of the description as if it is the only sentence you will see. Ask:

> Does this give a cautious reader a specific reason to click rather than the next result?

If the answer is no, the first sentence needs rewriting before the second is considered.

### Meta description reference table by page type

| Page type | Primary profile | Description structure | Character target |
|---|---|---|---|
| City management page | B | Honest income comparison framing + curiosity gap | 140–150 |
| City management page | A | Performance proof + switching ease signal | 130–145 |
| National service page | B/A | Differentiator + scale proof + low-friction CTA | 145–155 |
| Calculator page | C/B | What makes this estimate different + time to complete | 130–145 |
| Guide page | Matched | Honest framing of what the guide covers + who it is for | 130–150 |
| County hub page | B | County-level income signal + cities covered | 135–150 |

### Common description failures — never use

- "Stayful provides professional Airbnb management in [City]. Get in touch today."
- "Find out more about our Airbnb management services in [City]."
- Any description that ends with "Contact us today" or "Get started now"
- Any description that is generic enough to apply to any competitor

## H1 vs Meta Title — critical distinction

The curiosity-gap rule applies to **meta titles**, not to H1s.

- **Meta titles:** require a curiosity gap. They compete with 9 other SERP entries; the gap is what creates the click decision.
- **H1s:** require accuracy and descriptiveness. Discover penalises curiosity-gap H1s (see `CORE__03_content__ai-citation-and-discover-readiness.md` — Discover headline integrity rule).

A title and H1 can — and often should — differ:

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Meta title | "Airbnb Management Leeds — What Your Property Could Realistically Earn" |
| H1 | "Short-term letting in Leeds — what your property could realistically earn" |

The title carries the curiosity hook for the SERP. The H1 carries the descriptive accuracy for Discover and AI extraction.

---

## Cross-page consistency

The meta title and description for a given page should be revisited:

- Quarterly, alongside the dateModified refresh
- Whenever business facts referenced in the description change (e.g. fee structure, occupancy figure)
- Whenever Phase 1 audit identifies a CTR underperformance vs. the keyword's typical CTR by position

A page ranking at position 4 with CTR below 4% is signalling that the title and description are losing the click decision. That's a Phase 2 Section K candidate even if the body content is strong.

---

## How this applies to non-Stayful websites

The rules are domain-neutral.

**E-commerce:**
- Title: "[Product name] — [specific benefit / unique angle] | [Brand]"
- Description: "[First-sentence moral contract about what makes this product worth clicking] — [supporting detail]"

**SaaS:**
- Title: "[Tool category] for [audience] — [specific outcome or differentiator]"
- Description: "[First-sentence acknowledgement of reader's actual situation] — [what they'll get from clicking]"

**Publisher:**
- Title: "[Topic] — [what the article uniquely covers]"
- Description: "[First-sentence honest framing] — [scope of the article]"

In every case, the 55-character title limit, 150-character description limit, profile-matched construction, and first-sentence moral contract rules apply.
