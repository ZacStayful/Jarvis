# Objections Framework and FAQ Lead Language Principle

**Scope: Universal framework. Specific Stayful instances live in `STAYFUL__objections__six-mandatory.md` and `STAYFUL__faq__lead-language-tables.md`.**

Every business has a finite set of mandatory objections that must be addressed on every commercial page. Every page must use the language readers actually speak, not the language the business uses internally.

---

## The Objections Framework

### Step 1 — Identify the mandatory objections for your business

The mandatory objections are the questions that EVERY interested reader has, not just the ones who ask out loud. Most readers will leave without asking if these objections aren't addressed proactively.

Identifying them requires:

- Lead-conversion conversation logs
- Sales call recordings (the questions asked in the first 10 minutes)
- Support tickets from the first 30 days post-purchase
- Competitor FAQ sections (objections their readers raise)
- PAA boxes for the primary keyword cluster

The set is usually 4–7 objections. Fewer than 4 and you've missed some. More than 7 and you've split similar objections that should be combined.

### Step 2 — Classify each objection

For each mandatory objection:

- **Critical (body copy required):** must appear in body copy, not buried in FAQ. These are the objections that decide the click vs. bounce moment.
- **Standard (FAQ acceptable):** can live in FAQ if visibly accessible.
- **Edge case (FAQ note acceptable):** brief acknowledgement is sufficient.

For Stayful, the six mandatory objections classify as:

| Objection | Class |
|---|---|
| What happens in slow months / worst-case | Critical (body copy) |
| Can you guarantee the income | Critical (body copy near CTA) |
| Do I lose control of my property | Critical (body copy near CTA) |
| What if figures aren't accurate | Standard |
| What's the total fee load | Standard |
| What about bad guests and damage | Standard |

### Step 3 — Decide the exact language for critical objections

For Critical-class objections, the exact phrasing matters. It must be:

- Honest (acknowledging the legitimate concern)
- Specific (giving a real answer, not deflecting)
- Reassuring without overpromising
- Placed where the reader is asking the question internally

Stayful's exact language for Objections 2 and 3 is documented in `STAYFUL__objections__six-mandatory.md`.

### Step 4 — Cover the rest in FAQ

The remaining objections go in FAQ, but using the FAQ Lead Language principle below.

## The FAQ Lead Language Principle

> Every FAQ question must be written in the language of the reader, not the language of the business.

This is one of the highest-leverage rules in the engine. FAQs in business language fail to match PAA, fail to extract for AI Overviews, and feel impersonal to the reader. FAQs in lead language match queries verbatim, extract cleanly, and read like genuine conversation.

### The pattern

| Use this (lead language) | Not this (business language) |
|---|---|
| "What if I have a really bad month?" | "What happens during periods of lower occupancy?" |
| "Can you guarantee how much I'll earn?" | "What income assurance does Stayful provide?" |
| "Can I still use my own property?" | "What is Stayful's owner access policy?" |
| "How quickly can you get it listed?" | "What is the onboarding timeline?" |
| "What if a guest damages something?" | "What is Stayful's damage management policy?" |

Full Stayful tables: `STAYFUL__faq__lead-language-tables.md`.

### Why this matters

1. **PAA matching.** Google's PAA boxes show questions in the language people actually use. An FAQ question phrased in business language doesn't match the PAA box; the page misses the snippet opportunity.

2. **AI Overview citation.** AI extractors prefer natural-language questions because they map cleanly to user prompts. "Can you guarantee how much I'll earn?" is closer to "can I earn a guaranteed amount" than "What income assurance does Stayful provide?"

3. **Voice search.** Voice queries are conversational. Lead-language FAQs surface more often in voice results.

4. **Reader trust.** A FAQ in business language reads as if the brand wrote it for itself. A FAQ in lead language reads as if the brand is anticipating the reader's actual concerns.

### FAQ question format rules

1. **Always use the lead's phrasing verbatim** where possible
2. **Never use business jargon** in the question trigger
3. **PAA questions harvested from Google must appear verbatim**
4. **One question per FAQ item** — never combine two questions into one trigger
5. **Never start an FAQ answer with "Great question"** or any affirmation
6. **Start FAQ answers with the direct answer**, then the explanation

### Example — FAQ answer format

**Wrong:**

> Q: How quickly can you get it listed?
> A: That's a great question! At Stayful, we pride ourselves on our fast and efficient onboarding process. Our team works tirelessly to ensure that your property is listed on all major platforms in record time. Typically, our onboarding process takes 7–14 days.

**Right:**

> Q: How quickly can you get it listed?
> A: 7–14 days from your onboarding call to live listings on Airbnb, Booking.com, VRBO and Google. The variance comes down to photography scheduling and how much existing setup is already in place.

The right version: direct answer first (7–14 days), then the mechanism (what determines speed). No affirmation, no padding.

## The objection-to-FAQ mapping

Every Critical objection must appear in body copy. The corresponding FAQ entry then deepens the answer.

Every Standard objection appears in FAQ in lead language.

The page structure ensures:

| Reader has objection in mind | Page surfaces |
|---|---|
| "What if it's worse than they say?" | Worst-case figure in body copy (Critical objection — slow months) |
| "Can you actually guarantee it?" | Honest "no guarantee" framing near CTA (Critical objection — income guarantee) |
| "Do I lose control of my property?" | Control statement near CTA (Critical objection — property control) |
| "But what about [specific edge case]?" | FAQ entry in their words (Standard objections) |

---

## Why business language sneaks in

Default writer instinct is to write in the language the business uses internally:

- "Onboarding process" instead of "getting it listed"
- "Owner access policy" instead of "can I still use my property"
- "Income assurance" instead of "can you guarantee my income"

This happens because internal teams use the formal phrasing daily. Pages drift toward that vocabulary unless actively reviewed for it.

Fix: re-read every FAQ question. Ask: "Would a reader type this query into Google?" If they would type a different phrasing, rewrite to match.

## How to identify lead language for a new business

If you don't yet have a documented lead language list:

1. **Pull live sales call transcripts.** What words do leads use for the service, the alternative, the worry, the outcome?
2. **Pull live chat transcripts.** Even shorter — questions get written in raw form.
3. **Read support tickets from the first 30 days.** Questions there are usually in raw lead language.
4. **Read PAA boxes for primary keyword.** Google has aggregated lead language for you.
5. **Read forum threads (Reddit, industry forums).** Forum language is closest to true lead language.

Build a "use this / not this" table. Update it quarterly as language shifts.

## How this applies to non-Stayful websites

The framework adapts directly.

**E-commerce — typical objections:**
- "Will it actually fit?"
- "Can I return it?"
- "Will it arrive in time?"
- "Is the quality as good as it looks?"
- "Is the price fair vs. alternatives?"

**SaaS — typical objections:**
- "Will my team actually use it?"
- "Will it integrate with what we have?"
- "What if we don't get value?"
- "How long until it pays back?"
- "What does cancellation look like?"

**Service business — typical objections:**
- "How long does it take?"
- "What does it actually cost (all-in)?"
- "What if it doesn't work?"
- "What's the catch?"
- "Are you the right size for my project?"

The lead language principle applies in every case. Sales transcripts surface the phrasing.
