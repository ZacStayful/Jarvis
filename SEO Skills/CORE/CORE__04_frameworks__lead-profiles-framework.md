# Lead Profiles Framework

**Scope: Universal framework. Specific Stayful instances live in `STAYFUL__lead-profiles__A-to-F.md`.**

Six profiles every page should serve simultaneously, structured by priority. The specific profiles change per business; the framework is universal.

---

## Why profiles matter

A page written for "everyone" converts no one. A page primarily written for one profile, with secondary support for adjacent profiles, converts.

The framework forces explicit choice: which profile is this page primarily for? What is the primary profile's search intent, emotional state, copy tone, and key proof point? Once that's defined, every subsequent decision (H1, intro structure, FAQ language, CTA placement) is easier.

---

## The framework — six profile categories

Adapt these labels to your business. The structure stays the same.

### Profile A — Primary commercial / urgent

The reader who already needs the service and is actively shopping. Highest conversion rate per visit. Smallest segment, but commercially the most important.

**Define for your business:**
- What is their immediate pain?
- What search queries do they type?
- What's their emotional state on arrival?
- What's the key proof point that wins them?
- What's the copy tone?

### Profile B — Primary considered / weighing alternatives

The reader who is considering the service but hasn't decided. **Largest organic lead segment for most businesses — pages should be built around this profile first.**

Two common emotional entry points:
- Rational evaluator (analytically comparing options)
- Frustration-driven (motivated by a bad experience with the alternative)

**Define:**
- What's the alternative they're weighing?
- What's the search query that surfaces this page?
- What's the emotional state — typically guarded?
- What objections must be addressed?
- What proof reduces the perceived risk of switching?

### Profile C — Validating before purchase / commit

The reader who is in pre-purchase research. Often optimistic, less risk-averse than B, may carry unrealistic expectations.

**Define:**
- What are they validating?
- What unrealistic expectation must be gently grounded?
- What honest figure must be shown to anchor their thinking?
- What's the bridge from validation to commitment?

### Profile D — Transitional / impatient

A reader for whom the service is a stopgap, not a long-term commitment. Often the wrong fit for premium service.

**Define:**
- What's their actual goal (the service is incidental)?
- What honest limitation must be acknowledged?
- What brief FAQ answer is sufficient?
- What disqualifier is appropriate?

### Profile E — Aspirational / structurally limited

A reader with a vision the business technically serves, but where structural factors make profitability or fit unlikely. Common in property: residential mortgages preventing STL.

**Define:**
- What's the vision they're pursuing?
- What structural reality must be honestly stated?
- How to acknowledge respect for the vision while providing the honest information?

### Profile F — Marginal commercial / structurally unprofitable

A reader whose situation makes the service economically unviable for both sides. FAQ note usually sufficient.

**Define:**
- What's the marginal economics that breaks the deal?
- What's the brief honest response?

### Profile modifier — Portfolio / scaled buyer

A reader with multiple instances of the use case (portfolio landlord, multi-site operator, agency buyer). Can fall into any of A–F but converts more easily.

**Define:**
- What language register matches their commercial sophistication?
- What proof points scale appropriately?
- How does the page acknowledge multi-instance buyers without alienating single-instance ones?

## How to use profiles in page strategy

### Matching page type to primary profile

| Page type | Primary profile | Secondary profiles served |
|---|---|---|
| Service / management pages | B (largest segment) | A and C through FAQ |
| Calculator / data landing pages | C | B through worst-case framing |
| Comparison / vs pages | A | B through performance data |
| Guides and supporting pages | Match guide topic to profile intent | — |

### H1 and H2 language by profile

**Profile A:**
- "Short let management company in [City] — what Stayful handles"
- "Why [City] short let landlords switch to Stayful"

**Profile B:**
- "Short-term letting in [City] — is it worth switching from a long-term tenancy?"
- "How much could you earn short-letting your [City] property?"

**Profile C:**
- "Short-term let income in [City] — what a property could realistically earn"
- "Buying a property to short let in [City] — honest income figures"

The same page can address multiple profiles, but the H1 prioritises the primary profile.

## Search intent — how to translate profiles into keyword strategy

Each profile has a typical search query pattern:

| Profile | Query pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| A | "[service] company [location]" / "switch [service]" | "Airbnb management company Leeds" |
| B | "is [service] worth it" / "[service] vs [alternative]" | "is short-term letting worth it Leeds" |
| C | "[outcome] [location]" / "income estimate [location]" | "Airbnb income Leeds" |
| D | "temporary [service]" / "[service] while [other situation]" | "short let while selling" |
| E | "[service] when [structural condition]" | "short let with residential mortgage" |
| F | "[service] for [marginal segment]" | "rent to rent Airbnb management" |

The page primary keyword should match the primary profile's query pattern.

## Building the profile definitions for a new business

Use the template `TEMPLATE__lead-profiles-blank.md` to define A–F (or however many profiles apply) for a new business.

The minimum information for each profile:

1. **Who they are** (one sentence)
2. **Search intent** (typical query patterns)
3. **Emotional state on arrival** (guarded? urgent? optimistic? frustrated?)
4. **What the page must show them** (the core proof and information)
5. **Copy tone** (direct / calm / honest / efficient)
6. **Key proof point** (the single most persuasive fact for this profile)

Without these six fields defined, a page cannot be properly built. The page may have content, but it has no audience anchor.

---

## Profile evolution

Profiles change as the market changes. Quarterly review:

- Are the primary profiles still primary? (Has the lead mix shifted?)
- Are there new emerging profiles?
- Have any profiles become structurally less important?
- Are the secondary profiles still relevant to current pages?

If lead data shows a profile drift, the page strategy needs updating before the gap shows up in conversion data.

---

## Why this framework matters for SEO (not just conversion)

Profile-matched pages convert better, which improves engagement metrics (time on page, scroll depth, low bounce), which feeds back into ranking signals.

A page written for "all readers" has weaker engagement signals than a page written for a specific profile. Both pages can have the same content depth, but the targeted page produces clearer reader-fit signals — Google rewards this.

The frame "who is this page primarily for?" is as much an SEO question as a conversion question.

## How this applies to non-Stayful websites

The six-profile framework adapts to any business:

**E-commerce:**
- A — Active buyer ready to purchase
- B — Comparing to alternatives
- C — Researching before first purchase
- D — Returning for replacement
- E — Aspirational browser
- F — Wrong fit (price-sensitive shopper for premium product)

**SaaS:**
- A — Active evaluation, ready to trial
- B — Comparing tools for a specific need
- C — Researching whether to invest in software at all
- D — Looking for a temporary solution
- E — Aspirational but pre-funded
- F — Too small to be profitable customer

**Service business:**
- A — Needs the service immediately
- B — Considering the service vs DIY
- C — Researching for future need
- D — One-off use case
- E — Aspirational but structurally wrong
- F — Below minimum economic engagement

The framework adapts; the principle (define profiles explicitly, match pages to primary profile) stays the same.
