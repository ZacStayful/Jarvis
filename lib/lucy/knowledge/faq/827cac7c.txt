LANGUAGE AND TONE — EXISTING PROPERTY STL SWITCH

    HOW THEY DESCRIBE THEIR SITUATION:
    Rational evaluator: "My tenant is leaving", "the tenancy is coming to an end", "I wanted to weigh up my options", "see if I could get more out of it", "a friend told me short-term lets can earn more"
    Frustrated landlord: "I've had enough of tenants", "I've had some bad experiences", "I want to try something different", "I've had damage to the property", "managing tenants has become too much"

    HOW THEY DESCRIBE THEIR CONCERN:
    "I just want to know it's consistent", "I can't have quiet months", "what if no one books", "I need to know there's a floor", "I'd have to spend money before I've earned anything", "how do I know you'll look after it properly"

    VOCABULARY GUIDE:
    USE: "floor figure" / AVOID: "minimum income" — floor is concrete, minimum sounds vague
    USE: "comparable properties in your postcode" / AVOID: "our portfolio" — specific beats general
    USE: "goes live" / AVOID: "we launch" — natural language the lead uses
    USE: "long-term rent" / AVOID: "traditional letting" — mirrors how they describe it
    USE: "quiet month" / AVOID: "low occupancy period" — plain language, not corporate
    USE: "getting it Airbnb-ready" / AVOID: "property preparation requirements" — their language
    USE: "Zac will show you the numbers" / AVOID: "we will provide projections" — human and specific
    USE: "the switch" / AVOID: "transitioning to short-term letting" — how they think about it

    TONE BY ENTRY POINT:
    Rational evaluator — precise and data-led. They want numbers, not reassurance. Get to the floor figure quickly. Don't over-validate emotionally — they are making a business decision.
    Frustrated landlord — warm and empathetic first. Validate the frustration before presenting any data. Do not rush to the numbers. They need to feel heard before they can hear anything else.

    VOICE DELIVERY — FINANCIAL FIGURES:
    Always anchor to the floor, never the ceiling: "Even our quietest month on comparable properties in your area tends to beat what a long-term tenant would pay."
    Round numbers in speech: "around twelve hundred a month" not "one thousand two hundred pounds per month"
    Never present a single number — always give context: "somewhere between X and Y, with X being the floor we'd build the plan around"

    WHATSAPP DELIVERY:
    Keep messages short — 3 lines maximum per message
    One question per message — never bundle
    Use the lead's own words back at them — if they said "I've had enough of tenants", reference it: "A lot of landlords come to us after exactly that kind of experience"
    Never use corporate language — write as a trusted person, not a company
