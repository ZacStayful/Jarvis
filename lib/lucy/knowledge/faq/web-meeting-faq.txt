— EARNINGS & PRICING —

Q: How much can I earn from my property? / What can I make on short-term letting? / What's the income potential?
A: It really depends on the specifics — location, bedrooms, finish, season all play a part — so the honest answer is that Zac runs the actual floor and average figures for your property in the web meeting. As a guide, well-set-up short lets in most UK markets earn meaningfully more than a long let on the same property, but you want the property-specific numbers, not a generic one.

Q: What's the management fee? / How much do you charge? / What's your commission?
A: Our management fee is 15 percent plus VAT of gross bookings, full end-to-end. If you're ready to come onboard this month and the property is ready to go live, we've got a discount running at 13 percent plus VAT, and that rate is locked in for the life of the contract.

Q: How is the net figure calculated? / What deductions come out before I get paid? / How does the net work?
A: The net figure is what lands in your bank after our 15 percent plus VAT fee, platform commissions like Airbnb's host fee, and VAT where applicable. Cleaning isn't a deduction from you because it's billed to the guest, so the net you see really is what you receive each month.

Q: How does short-term letting compare to long-term renting? / Is short let better than a long let?
A: On the same property, short-term letting typically grosses two to three times what a long let would, and the net is still well ahead once our fee and turnover costs are factored in. The other big difference is flexibility — you keep access for personal use, and you can switch back to a long let if your situation ever changes.

— PROPERTY USE & MANAGEMENT —

Q: Can I still use the property myself? / Can I block dates for personal use? / Can I stay in it?
A: Yes, the property stays yours to use whenever you want. You just block the dates in the calendar and we work around them — most owners use the property a few weeks a year and let it the rest of the time.

Q: What if I need access to my own property? / How do I get in while it's being let? / Can I access it whenever I want?
A: Of course — it's your property and you can have access whenever you need it. All we ask is 72 hours notice before access, so we can coordinate around any guest bookings and make sure there's no clash with someone mid-stay. Give us those three days and we'll get it arranged for you.

Q: What does full management actually mean? / What do you do for me? / What's included?
A: Full management means we handle everything end-to-end — listings, photography, dynamic pricing, guest enquiries, bookings, check-ins, cleaning, linen, maintenance coordination, reviews, and your monthly payout. The only thing you do is approve the setup and check the statement when the payout lands.

Q: How do I know what's happening if I'm not local? / Managing it remotely worries me / I don't live near the property, how does that work?
A: That's exactly what full management is built for — you never need to be hands-on or nearby. We handle check-in with smart locks, our local supplier network covers cleaning and maintenance on the ground, and you get your own private Slack channel with the team plus an out-of-hours line for anything urgent. Most of our owners aren't local and plenty are overseas, so the whole thing is designed to run without you ever being there.

Q: What happens if a guest damages the property? / How am I protected from damage? / Is there cover for guests?
A: Every guest stay is covered by short-term-let insurance — we use Guardhog and Pikl, the two specialist providers for this market. They cover guest-caused damage, accidental damage, and liability, so you're not relying on a standard home policy that would exclude paying guests.

Q: What about the quiet months? / What's the worst case? / What if it doesn't book?
A: We always work to a floor figure rather than a ceiling — the floor is the quietest realistic month based on what comparable properties in your area actually earn. It's much easier to predict than people expect, and that's the number Zac walks you through in the meeting so you can plan around it confidently.

Q: Do you guarantee income? / Can you guarantee rent? / Is there a minimum payout?
A: We do offer guaranteed rent, but it's selective — only on properties where the numbers stack up for both sides. We'd rather be honest about what works than promise a guarantee on a property that doesn't suit it, so Zac will tell you straight in the meeting whether yours qualifies.

Q: How do guests get into the property? / How does access work? / Do they meet someone?
A: Most properties use a smart lock or a key safe with a unique code per booking, so guests can check in at any time without anyone meeting them. We send the instructions ahead of arrival and the code resets between every stay, so previous guests can't get back in.

Q: What ongoing costs do I have? / What do I keep paying for as the owner?
A: Your main ongoing costs are utilities, internet, council tax or business rates, building insurance, and any service charge if it's leasehold. Cleaning and platform fees are covered by guests, and our management fee comes out of bookings, so there's nothing extra you have to fund.

Q: Which platforms do you list on? / Where does it get advertised?
A: We list every property on Airbnb, Booking.com, and through our own direct booking channel. Around 40 percent of bookings come direct, which is unusual in the industry and means you're not dependent on any one platform.

Q: Can I see my bookings and statements? / How do I know what's happening?
A: You get full visibility through a channel manager — every booking, every guest, every price, in real time. Statements land every month with full transparency, so you can see exactly how the income is built up.

Q: How do you vet guests? / Are guests checked? / What stops bad guests booking?
A: Every guest is vetted before booking — government ID verification, deposit checks, and screening against the red-flag patterns we see in this industry. We hold a damage deposit on every stay, and we don't accept under-25 local bookings without further checks.

Q: How does the pricing work? / Do you change the price? / What's dynamic pricing?
A: We use smart data to set the price and then keep optimising it day by day — it responds to real demand, your booking patterns, guest reviews, seasonality, local events, and how far ahead the booking is. So you're never under-pricing a peak weekend or sitting too high in a quiet midweek; the rate flexes to earn the most the market will pay at any given moment, rather than sitting at one static figure.

— CONTRACT & TERMS —

Q: How long is the contract? / What's the minimum term?
A: The initial term is six months, and after that it moves to a three-month rolling notice period. The reason for the fixed six is that it takes that long to build real momentum and reach the figures we project — a new listing needs time to establish bookings, reviews, and ranking before it performs at its best.

Q: What's the notice period after the first six months? / How do I end the contract?
A: After the six-month fixed term you're on a rolling three-month notice — you can give notice at any point and we wind down cleanly over three months. It's not a long lock-in; the fixed period just gives the listing time to build momentum and reach the figures we project.

Q: Can I sell the property while it's being managed? / What if I want to sell?
A: You can sell at any point — we just work around viewings and coordinate with your estate agent to keep them from clashing with bookings. If the sale completes, we transfer the listings to the new owner if they want to continue, or wind down cleanly.

Q: Are there any upfront fees? / Do I pay anything to get started?
A: There are no fees from us to get started — no setup fee, no onboarding charge, no listing fee. The only upfront spend is on the property itself if it needs anything to be guest-ready, like furnishing or finishing touches, and that sits with you as the owner. You never pay Stayful anything before bookings start — we only earn when you earn.

Q: Why is the minimum term six months? / Can I do a shorter contract?
A: The minimum is six months because that's how long it takes to build momentum and reach the figures we've projected — a new listing has to establish bookings, reviews, and ranking before it performs at its best. We don't charge anything to onboard you; the only setup costs are whatever the property itself needs to be guest-ready, and those sit with you as the owner. After that it's a rolling three-month notice, so the only real commitment is the first six.

Q: How long does it take to go live? / When will guests start booking?
A: Most properties go live within two to four weeks of signing. The variables are usually photography scheduling, any furnishing top-ups, and getting insurance and access set up — none of which are huge blockers.

— FURNISHING & SETUP —

Q: Does the property need to be furnished a certain way? / What are the furnishing requirements?
A: Properties need to feel comfortable and well-presented — that means a proper bed setup, decent linen, good lighting, a working kitchen, and the basics like crockery and Wi-Fi. As a rough cost guide, furnishing a one-bed runs around three to five thousand and a two-bed around five to seven, and we give you a supplier list if you don't already have one in mind.

Q: What's the onboarding process? / What happens once I sign? / Walk me through getting started.
A: Once you sign, we book a property viewing, sort the photography, write the listings across every platform, and get the insurance and access setup arranged. We coordinate the whole thing for you with a single point of contact in your Slack channel, so it's a few decisions and approvals on your side rather than a project to manage.

Q: Can you help me furnish it? / Will you furnish the property for me?
A: We don't furnish the property for you, but you won't be doing it alone — we point you to trusted local interior suppliers and tell you exactly what works for short-term letting. So you get a clear brief on what to buy and what to skip, without having to figure it out from scratch.

Q: What about council tax versus business rates? / How does the tax band change?
A: If you let the property for 70 nights or more in a year and make it available for 140, you can apply to switch from council tax to business rates. For most smaller properties that means you qualify for Small Business Rate Relief, which is effectively zero — so it can be a significant saving.

Q: What does the management fee actually cover? / What do I get for 15 percent?
A: That covers everything we do — listings, dynamic pricing, guest management, check-ins, cleaning coordination, maintenance handling, statements, and your dedicated support channel. There are no add-on fees or services billed separately; one number, full service.

Q: How does cleaning work? / Who pays for cleaning?
A: Cleaning is built into the guest booking — guests pay a cleaning fee that covers the turnover, and our local cleaners handle everything between stays. So it doesn't come out of your net, and you don't need to organise cleaners yourself.

Q: What's the income difference between year one and an established property?
A: Year one for a brand new listing usually runs at around 70 to 80 percent of mature-property earnings — there's a ramp-up while you build reviews, ranking, and direct repeat guests. By year two most properties hit their proper earning level, which is the number we project forward from.

The honest test is whether your time is worth more than the management fee — for most owners it is, by some distance.

— SWITCHING & OPERATIONS —

Q: I'm with another management company — can I switch to you?
A: Switching is straightforward — we coordinate the handover, take over the listings, and make sure no booked guests are disrupted in the transition. Most owners switch because of communication or payout issues, and the first thing you'll notice with us is the dedicated Slack channel and the reliable payout window.

Q: What happens if something goes wrong out of hours? / How are emergencies handled?
A: We have an out-of-hours line that handles emergencies 24/7 — burst pipes, lockouts, heating failures, anything urgent. For routine work we use our approved local supplier network, and you see every job on your statement so there are no surprises.

Q: Can I use my own cleaners or my own maintenance team?
A: If you've got a cleaner or handyman you already trust, we can bring them into the supplier network as long as they meet our standards and pricing. Most owners end up using ours because the coordination is simpler, but it's not a requirement.

Q: How do you keep me in the loop? / How does communication work?
A: Every owner gets their own private Slack channel with the Stayful team, so you can ask anything in real time and see exactly what's happening with your property. No chasing, no waiting on email, no wondering if a message got through.

— BUYING & COMPLIANCE —

Q: I'm thinking of buying a new property — can you help me work out if it's worth it?
A: We're happy to advise on what works for short-term letting before you buy — location, layout, parking, planning permissions. Zac can look at any property you're considering and give you a realistic projection so you know what it'll earn before you commit.

Q: Do I need permission from my mortgage lender? / Can I short-let with a standard mortgage?
A: Most standard residential mortgages don't allow short-term letting, so you'll need to either get permission from your existing lender or move to an STL-friendly mortgage. We can point you to brokers who specialise in this, but the permission piece is something you'd handle with your lender directly.

Q: What insurance do I need? / Is normal home insurance enough?
A: You need short-term-let-specific insurance because standard home cover excludes paying guests. The two market specialists are Guardhog and Pikl — we'll recommend the right one for your property and help you get a quote during onboarding.

Q: My property is leasehold — do I need permission from the freeholder?
A: If the property is leasehold, you'll need to check the lease — most allow short-term letting, but some restrict it and a few require freeholder consent. We can review the lease language with you and flag anything worth clarifying before you commit.

Q: What about tax? / Does the Furnished Holiday Let regime still apply?
A: Short-term letting income is taxable like any other rental income, and historically the Furnished Holiday Let regime offered some tax advantages, though that area is changing. Your accountant is the right person to walk you through what currently applies — we can share a guidance note we use with owners, but we're not tax advisers.

Q: What kind of mortgage do I need for a short-let property? / Are there STL mortgages?
A: If you need a new mortgage specifically for short-term letting, there's a small but growing set of specialist lenders — typically commercial or holiday-let products, with deposits in the 25 to 35 percent range. We can refer you to brokers who place this kind of business, but the underwriting is theirs not ours.
