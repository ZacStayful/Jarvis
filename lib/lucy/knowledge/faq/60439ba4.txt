PROFILE: Existing Property — Considering STL Switch
    TIER: Tier 1 — Highest Conversion
    VOLUME: Primary organic segment — highest volume of all profiles
    AGENT TREATMENT: Lead is half-decided or curious on arrival. Do not over-qualify. Focus on understanding their current situation, then book the web meeting.

    WHO THEY ARE:
    This lead currently long-lets a property and is weighing up a switch to short-term letting. They arrive in one of two emotional states depending on what triggered the enquiry, but both land in the same conversation. They are either:

    Entry Point A — Rational Evaluator: Their tenant is leaving and rather than find a new one, they are weighing up short-term letting as a higher-income alternative. They are analytical and motivated by income potential. They are not frustrated — they are opportunistic.

    Entry Point B — Frustrated Landlord: They have had difficult tenants, property damage, or management hassle. They are done with long-term letting and want out. The frustration is the trigger, but the income potential is still the argument that converts them.

    In both cases, Lucy does not need to know which entry point applies. The lead will explain their situation naturally when asked. Do not ask which sub-type they are — let them tell you.

    PROPERTY SITUATION:
    Leads in this profile often have an existing tenant still in place when they enquire, or they say the property is becoming vacant soon. This is usually the trigger for the enquiry — the tenancy ending or a bad experience prompting a review. Do not ask about the property situation directly. Ask them to explain their current situation and they will provide this naturally.

    BEDROOM COUNT: Available in Monday from the lead record. Do not ask.
    LOCATION: Varies — does not affect the approach.

    EMOTIONAL STATE ON ARRIVAL:
    Half-decided or curious. They have not committed to STL but have made the mental move toward it. They are not cold — they enquired for a reason. Match their energy: if they are analytical, be precise; if they are frustrated, validate first before presenting data.

    WHAT DRIVES THEM:
    - Rational evaluator: income increase potential — they are comparing STL revenue against long-term rent and want to know if the switch is worth it
    - Frustrated landlord: relief from bad tenant experiences — the income increase is a bonus on top of wanting out of long-term letting

    CRITICAL DEADLINE — NOTE IN MONDAY:
    This profile often has a hard deadline: the date the tenant is moving out. When this comes up in conversation, Lucy must note it in the call summary on Monday. This is the urgency driver and should inform how quickly the web meeting is booked and followed up.

    WHAT LUCY NEVER ASKS THIS PROFILE:
    - Why they want to move away from long-term letting — it does not matter. They enquired. Focus on the web meeting.
    - What bad experience they had — does not matter. Let them share if they want to, but do not probe.
    - What income they are hoping to achieve — covered in the web meeting.
    - What type of property or how many bedrooms — already in Monday.

    LUCY'S OBJECTIVE WITH THIS PROFILE:
    Ask them to explain their current situation. Listen. Validate what they share. Book the web meeting. The web meeting is where Stayful shows them the specific income projection, the floor figure, and the management plan for their property.
